Method selector · decide before calculating

One sample mean?
Probability about X̄ → Ch 7. Confidence interval → Ch 8. Hypothesis test → Ch 9. Then ask: σ known = Z; σ unknown = t.
One sample proportion?
Sampling probability → Ch 7. CI / sample size → Ch 8. Hypothesis test → Ch 9. Use the proportion standard error and Z route.
Two groups?
Means independent → known σ Z or pooled t in Ch 10. Same people / matched pairs → paired t. Two proportions → Z. Two variances → F.
One X predicting Y?
Simple linear regression, Ch 13. Decode b0, b1, r², SSE/SSR/SST, sYX, t/F and residual/prediction information.
Several X variables predicting Y?
Multiple regression core, Ch 14. Use k predictors, adjusted r², overall F and model-significance hypotheses.
Tail?
H1 contains < → lower. H1 contains > → upper. H1 contains ≠ → two-tail. Tail comes from H1, never from the observed sample result.
Fast discrimination: parameter tells you the family; design tells you independent vs paired; knowledge of σ tells you Z vs t; H1 tells you the tail.

Chapter 7 · Sampling Distributions

Rebased from the existing A1 system into paper execution.

REUSED + REBASED
Core route

Sample mean

E(X̄)=μ    SE(X̄)=σ/√n

Write the random object first. A question about one observation uses σ; a question about an average of n observations uses σ/√n.

Trap: CLT makes the sampling distribution of X̄ approximately normal; it does not make the population normal.
Core route

Sample proportion

E(p)=π    SE(p)=√[π(1−π)/n]

For theoretical probabilities use π in the SE. Check the approximation conditions before standardising.

Trap: do not use the observed p in a theoretical probability question when π is supplied.
Bridge

Two independent sample means

E(X̄₁−X̄₂)=μ₁−μ₂
SE=√(σ₁²/n₁+σ₂²/n₂)

Preserve group order from start to finish. The expected difference is μ₁−μ₂, not zero unless the problem says so.

Finite population

FPC

FPC=√[(N−n)/(N−1)]

Use for sampling without replacement from a finite population when the sampling fraction is material (course rule: above 5%). FPC changes spread, not the centre.

Written frame

1Name the random object: X, X̄, p or X̄₁−X̄₂.
2Write centre and standard error.
3State why normal/CLT/proportion approximation applies.
4Translate below/above/between/inverse wording into the region.
5Standardise or solve the inverse boundary and interpret in context.

Primary basis: existing certified Chapter 7 system + official Chapter 7 deck/tutorial/formula references.

Chapter 8 · Confidence Interval Estimation

Point estimate ± critical value × standard error.

REUSED + REBASED

Mean · σ known

X̄ ± zα/2 · σ/√n

Use Z when the population standard deviation is known. State the confidence level, critical value, SE, margin of error and interval.

Mean · σ unknown

X̄ ± tα/2,n−1 · s/√n

Use Student t and df=n−1 when σ is unknown and s is used.

Population proportion

p ± zα/2 √[p(1−p)/n]

Interpret the interval as a range for the population proportion π, not for the observed sample proportion p.

Sample size

n ≥ z²α/2 σ²/e²
n ≥ z²α/2 π(1−π)/e²

Always round the required sample size up. For finite populations, apply the supplied FPC sample-size adjustment.

Finite population CI

ordinary SE × √[(N−n)/(N−1)]

Use when sampling without replacement from finite N and the sampling fraction exceeds the course threshold.

Bootstrap / totals

Bootstrap: repeated resampling with replacement from the observed sample builds an empirical sampling distribution when normality is doubtful. Population total point estimate is N·X̄; the total CI scales the mean estimate by N.

Interpretation sentence: “We are C% confident that the population [mean/proportion/total] lies between L and U.” Do not say that C% of individual observations lie inside the interval.

Chapter 9 · One-Sample Hypothesis Testing

Full A2 restoration: lower, upper and two-tail Z/t/proportion tests + power.

LECTURE SEQUENCE COMPLETE
H1 formTailCritical areap-value logic
parameter < valueLowerα in left tailP(test statistic or smaller)
parameter ≠ valueTwo-tailα/2 each tail2 × one-tail area beyond |stat|
parameter > valueUpperα in right tailP(test statistic or larger)

Mean · σ known

z = (X̄−μ₀)/(σ/√n)

Use the null value μ₀ in the numerator and the population σ in the denominator.

Mean · σ unknown

t = (X̄−μ₀)/(s/√n),   df=n−1

The Chapter 9 summary visually shows s/√n for the unknown-σ route; the parsed text can misread that symbol, so the build follows the rendered formula and Chapter 8/9 model.

One proportion

z = (p−π₀)/√[π₀(1−π₀)/n]

For the null test standard error use the hypothesised π₀.

Decision language

Reject H0 or fail/do not reject H0. Then write the evidence conclusion in context. Never “accept H0”.

Power family

Power = 1−β

Two-stage course method: find the critical X̄ boundary under H0, then calculate β under the stated true distribution. Power rises when n increases or the true parameter moves farther from the null; β rises when α falls, variance rises, n falls, or the true value moves closer to the null.

Common trap: the direction of the test is set by H1 before you see the sample result. Do not choose a tail because the observed X̄ happens to be above or below μ₀.

Primary basis: complete listed Chapter 9 lecture sequence, 3 Sep practical, Tutorial 9 Excel memo and supplied Chapter 9 one-page summary.

Chapter 10 · Two-Sample Tests + Intervals

Repaired v0.3.1: hypothesis tests and the assessed confidence-interval routes now live in one decision system.

RECOVERY BASELINE

Independent means · σ₁,σ₂ known

Z=[(X̄₁−X̄₂)−Δ₀]/√(σ₁²/n₁+σ₂²/n₂)
CI: (X̄₁−X̄₂) ± z*√(σ₁²/n₁+σ₂²/n₂)

Use the Z family only when both population SDs are known.

Independent means · equal unknown variances

s²p=[(n₁−1)s₁²+(n₂−1)s₂²]/(n₁+n₂−2)
SE=sp√(1/n₁+1/n₂)
t=[(X̄₁−X̄₂)−Δ₀]/SE
CI: (X̄₁−X̄₂) ± t*SE

Mark-bearing order: pooled variance → pooled SE → statistic/CI → df=n₁+n₂−2.

Paired / related means

t=(D̄−μD,0)/(sD/√n), df=n−1
CI: D̄ ± t*sD/√n

Reduce each pair to one difference. n is the number of pairs.

Two proportions · test

p̄=(x₁+x₂)/(n₁+n₂)
Z=[(p₁−p₂)−0]/√[p̄(1−p̄)(1/n₁+1/n₂)]

Equality-test denominator is pooled under H₀: π₁=π₂.

Two proportions · confidence interval

(p₁−p₂) ± z*√[p₁(1−p₁)/n₁ + p₂(1−p₂)/n₂]

Do not pool the CI standard error. The course deck uses the two observed sample proportions in the interval SE.

Two variances

F=s²large/s²small

For the module route, larger sample variance goes in the numerator. Match numerator and denominator df to the corresponding samples.

Unequal unknown variances

The Chapter 10 deck states that the unequal-variance t test is beyond the scope of this course. Recognise the case; do not invent a Welch route for this module unless later official material changes the instruction.

Effect size

Train interpretation rather than Bonett calculation: statistical significance asks whether evidence exists; practical significance asks whether the magnitude matters. The 18 September lecture uses approximate standardized-effect-size anchors of 0.2 small, 0.5 medium and 0.8 large.

Source discrepancy note: the Chapter 10 Excel p-value example displays p=0.0179, α=0.05 and the decision “Reject the null hypothesis”, but the printed Excel IF expression on that slide uses the comparator D22>D9. Those two elements conflict. This build follows the displayed decision and the module decision rule used elsewhere: reject when p-value<α.

Chapter 13 · Simple Linear Regression

Repaired v0.3.1: calculation, output interpretation, assumptions and correlation inference.

RECOVERY BASELINE

Fit the estimated line

b₁=SSXY/SSX
b₀=ȳ−b₁x̄
ŷ=b₀+b₁X

For small datasets the official deck gives the hand-calculator coefficient route. Keep the hat on predicted Y.

Interpret coefficients

Intercept: estimated mean Y when X=0, if X=0 is meaningful. Slope: estimated change in mean Y for a one-unit increase in X.

Sums of squares + fit

SST=SSR+SSE
r²=SSR/SST
sYX=√[SSE/(n−2)]

Use these identities to reconstruct deliberately omitted output cells.

ANOVA / slope inference

MSR=SSR
MSE=SSE/(n−2)
F=MSR/MSE
t=(b₁−β₁)/sb₁, df=n−2

For the usual slope test, H₀:β₁=0. The slope CI is b₁±t*sb₁.

Correlation inference

H₀:ρ=0; H₁:ρ≠0
t=(r−ρ)/√[(1−r²)/(n−2)], df=n−2

The deck uses r with the sign of b₁. Do not replace this with the slope t formula by accident.

Prediction + residual

ŷ=b₀+b₁x₀
residual=y−ŷ

Keep the residual sign. Avoid unsupported extrapolation beyond the observed X range.

Model

Yᵢ=β₀+β₁X₁ᵢ+…+βkXkᵢ+εᵢ

k is the number of predictors. Residual df = n−k−1.

ANOVA pieces

MSR=SSR/k
MSE=SSE/(n−k−1)
F=MSR/MSE

Overall F tests whether the predictor set has explanatory value as a model.

Adjusted r²

r²adj=1−(1−r²)(n−1)/(n−k−1)

Unlike ordinary r², adjusted r² penalises adding predictors through the df adjustment.

Overall hypotheses

H₀: β₁=β₂=…=βk=0
H₁: at least one βj≠0

Use F with df1=k and df2=n−k−1.

Individual coefficient t · formula-sheet anchor

t=(bⱼ−βⱼ)/sbⱼ

Written Practice Bank · v0.3.1

Short source-aligned prompts. Work on paper first; then open the answer.

Q1 · Ch 7 · Mean probability recognition

A population has μ=100, σ=10. A random sample of n=25 is selected. What distributional spread belongs to X̄?

Answer: SE=σ/√n=10/5=2. Centre remains 100. Do not use σ=10 for the sample mean.
Q2 · Ch 8 · Unknown-σ confidence interval route

You are given X̄, s and n, but no population σ. State the interval family and df.

Answer: Student-t interval, X̄±tα/2,n−1·s/√n, with df=n−1.
Q3 · Ch 9 · Tail recognition

H1: μ>500. Where is the rejection region and what happens to α?

Answer: Upper-tail test; place the full α in the right tail.
Q4 · Ch 9 · Power direction

With all else fixed, what happens to β and power when n increases?

Answer: β decreases and power=1−β increases.
Q5 · Ch 10 · Pooled independent means

n₁=8, X̄₁=42, s₁=4; n₂=15, X̄₂=34, s₂=5. Equal variances. Calculate s²p and df.

Answer: s²p=[7(16)+14(25)]/21=22; df=8+15−2=21.
Q6 · Ch 10 · Paired design trap

Twenty matched pairs are measured. What n and df belong to the paired t test?

Answer: n=20 differences; df=19. Do not use n=40.
Q7 · Ch 10 · F ratio

s₁=4 and s₂=7. Which variance belongs in the numerator for the module F-test route?

Answer: 7²=49 belongs in the numerator; 4²=16 in the denominator. Match df to the samples in that order.
Q8 · Ch 13 · Regression equation

b₀=1.45, b₁=0.074. Predict mean Y when X=8.

Answer: ŷ=1.45+0.074(8)=2.042. Keep the hat on predicted Y.
Q9 · Ch 13 · Missing r²

SSR=2.0535, SST=3.0025. Recover r².

Answer: r²=SSR/SST≈0.68393, so about 68.393% of the variation in Y is explained by variation in X through the estimated linear model.
Q10 · Ch 13 · Residual

Observed Y=3 and predicted ŷ=1.367? What is the residual rule?

Answer: residual=Y−ŷ. Keep the sign; do not take an absolute difference.
Q11 · Ch 14 · ANOVA reconstruction

n=64, k=9, SST=53895.51 and MSR=1455. Find SSR and residual df.

Answer: SSR=MSR×k=13095; residual df=64−9−1=54.
Q12 · Mixed · Choose the family

Two independent groups, population SDs unknown, equal population variances assumed. Which route?

Answer: Chapter 10 pooled-variance two-sample t route; df=n₁+n₂−2.
