III. Main distinctions / rules

1 - X versus X

X is one observation; X is a random-sample average.

Use σ for X and σ/√n for X.

2 - SD versus SE

σ describes variability among individuals; SE describes variability among possible statistics.

Larger n reduces SE, not the centre.

3 - Normal versus CLT

A valid independent random sample from a normal population gives normal X for any n.

For non-normal populations, sufficiently large n may justify the CLT.

4 - Ordinary SE versus FPC

Use ordinary SE with replacement or a negligible sampling fraction.

Without replacement from finite N, apply FPC when n/N > 0.05.

IV. Exam focus

  • Identify whether the question concerns X, X, p or X1-X2.
  • Write the centre and standard error before standardising.
  • Check normality, CLT, proportion or independence conditions.
  • Translate below, above, between and inverse wording into the correct normal region.
  • Interpret the final probability or boundary in the context of the sample statistic.

V. Common traps

  • Using σ instead of σ/√n for a sample mean.
  • Dividing by n instead of √n.
  • Confusing variance with standard deviation.
  • Saying the population becomes normal under the CLT.
  • Using the wrong tail or forgetting the complement.
  • Forgetting FPC above 5% without replacement.

1. Population formulas

μ = ∑i=1NXiN

σ2 = ∑i=1N(Xi-μ)2N

σ = ∑i=1N(Xi-μ)2N

Use N for a complete listed population, not n-1.

2. General exact sampling distributions

μT = ∑tP(t)

σT2 = ∑(t-μT)2P(t)

σT = ∑(t-μT)2P(t)

P(T=t)=frequency of ttotal number of equally likely samples

T is the statistic; t is one possible value. Use exact formulas when all values and probabilities are listed.

Nn = N!n!(N-n)!

ordered, with replacement: Nn
example: 42 = 16

3. Individual observation and sample mean

One individual observation (normal X)

Z = X-μσ

One person, item, time or observation: do not divide σ by √n.

Sampling distribution of the mean

E(X)=μX=μ

σX2=σ2n

σX=σ√n

X ∼ N(μ,σ2)  ⟹  X ∼ N(μ, σ2/n)

Notation on this sheet: N(mean, variance). Random independent sample required.

Z=X-μσ/√n

X=μ+Zσ√n

XL=μ+zLσ√n  ;  XU=μ+zUσ√n

μ±z*σ√n

Ordinary mean SE - use unless the finite-population correction applies.

4. Central Limit Theorem

X ≈ N(μ, σ2n)

Use when the CLT provides an adequate normal approximation.

Course guide: about n≈15 if fairly symmetric; about n≈30 for most populations; use greater caution with strong skewness or outliers.

5. Sample proportion

p=Xn

μp=π

σp=π(1-π)n

Ordinary proportion SE - use unless FPC applies. X is the success count.

p ≈ N(π, π(1-π)n)  ;  nπ≥5,   n(1-π)≥5

The second N parameter is the variance. Known π: use π in theoretical SE.

Z=p-ππ(1-π)/n

σ̂p=p(1-p)n

Unknown π and estimated SE requested: use observed p. No continuity correction.

6. Difference between two independent sample means

μX1-X2=μ1-μ2

σX1-X22=σ12n1+σ22n2

σX1-X2=σ12n1+σ22n2

X1-X2 ∼ N(μ1-μ2, σ12n1+σ22n2)

Z=(X1-X2)-(μ1-μ2)σ12/n1+σ22/n2

Applies with independence within/between random samples, known population SDs, and normal populations or sufficiently large samples.

Expected difference is μ1-μ2, not zero by default.

7. Finite-population correction

f=nN

FPC=N-nN-1

Use only without replacement from finite N when n/N > 0.05.

σX=σ√nN-nN-1

σp=π(1-π)nN-nN-1

FPC changes spread only; it does not change μ or π.
