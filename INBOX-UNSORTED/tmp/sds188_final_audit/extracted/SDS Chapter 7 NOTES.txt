1.1 Standard error
As with any distribution, the sampling distribution is described by an expected value and a variance.
We see that the different samples result in different means.
How do we measure the variation among the means ?
The usual measure of variation that we would have used, is the standard deviation. For the sampling
distribution, we use the standard error.

5.1 Question 1
The average time to complete a task is 727 milliseconds for Grade 3 students and 532 milliseconds for
Grade 5 students. The variances for the groups are 12 000 and 10 000 respectively. These times are
normally distributed. A random sample of 12 Grade 3 students and 14 Grade 5 students are selected.
1. What is the expected difference in mean times between Grade 3 and Grade 5 students?
2. What is the standard error of the difference in mean times between Grade 3 and Grade 5 stu-
dents?
3. What is the probability that the mean time of Grade 3 students exceeds the mean time of Grade
5 students with 150 milliseconds or more?

the sampling distribution of the mean is normally distributed.
(b) As the sample size gets large enough, the sampling distribution of the mean is approximately
normally distributed.
(c) The sampling distribution of the mean is normally distributed for all sample sizes.

5.3 Question 3
An orange juice producer buys oranges from a large orange grove that has one variety of orange.
The amount of juice squeezed from these oranges is approximately normally distributed, with a mean
of 120 ml and a standard deviation of 7 ml. Given a sample of 36 oranges.
1. What is the probability that the sample mean amount of juice will be at least 128 ml?
2. The probability is 74% that the sample mean amount of juice will be contained between which
two values symmetrically distributed around the population mean. Calculate these values.

or more female board directors?
