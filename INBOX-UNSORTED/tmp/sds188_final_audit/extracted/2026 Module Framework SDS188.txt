1.2 What are statistics and data science?
As a result of the information explosion, decisions are becoming increasingly based on large volumes of
data. Statistics is a way to get information from data. Statistics is concerned with the drawing of objective
conclusions from investigations where outcomes are subject to uncertainty and variability. Internationally,
Statistics is a compulsory service module in almost every study program leading towards a professional
qualification. People in this field are appointed amongst others as statisticians, data-mining experts, data
scientists, data engineers, and statistical analysts in the marketing, information, and management divisions
of companies.
Statistics is also helpful in predicting diseases, medical studies, genetic research, climate change research,
political campaigns, insurance investigations, manufacturing consumer goods, performing quality testing
and analysing the stock market. Statistics is the process of collecting, measuring, analysing, and communi-
cating information to aid decision making within an organisation. Students trained in Statistics develop the
ability for abstract reasoning, enabling them to make informed decisions based on statistical data.
Data Science is the combination of Statistics, Computer Science, and domain knowledge to build data-driven
products that help companies extract meaningful information from their data. Data scientists use data to
build products for businesses that aid decision making. Statistics and Data Science 188 introduces the fun-
damental concepts of data science, and present introductory statistical tools used in the data science pro-
fession.
1.3 Module aim
Statistics and Data Science 188 is an introductory course in practical data description and data analysis. The
objective of the module is to expose students to basic statistical concepts, exploratory data analysis, proba-
bility theory, hypothesis testing and regression analysis. All these topics will be applied to datasets using the
Excel computer software.
1.4 Module outcomes
After the successful completion of this module you will be able to:
•Define, solve, analyse, and interpret a linear programming problem (online topic for B-Accounting -
SAICA requirement).
•Understand basic statistical concepts and definitions.

4 Learning methodology
4.1 Method of instruction
Principles of adult learning, with an emphasis on blended learning, will be applied. You are invited to partic-
ipate interactively in this module and ask questions whenever anything is unclear .
There are three formal lecture periods and there is one practical session, consisting of two periods, every
week for each group.
Lectures will take place in lecture halls and will be used to explain the theory and applications of the differ-
ent topics in Statistics and Data Science. The practical sessions will take place in a computer venue where
lecturers will demonstrate the application of statistical concepts and the use of Excel.
Students are strongly encouraged to attempt the tutorial questions on their own before being shown how to
do them.
4.2 Practical sessions
Tutorials on every chapter will be made available for students to work on in their own time. The weekly
practical sessions will be used by lecturers to illustrate the methods used in solving the tutorial problems.
Attendance at all practical and lecture sessions are compulsory.
Memoranda in both English and Afrikaans for the tutorials will be made available after the relevant practical
sessions.
It is important to attempt the problems yourself before the answers are discussed in the practical sessions.

5.2 Assessment Further (AF)
The objective of the AF is to promote continuous learning throughout the module. It gives students the
opportunity to regularly test their knowledge, and quickly identify areas that require more work.
These take the form of regular online quizzes that students will have one week to complete. These assess-
ments AFS1 after the first semester and AFS2 in the second will each contribute 5% to the final mark. For each
semester , the lowest mark will be discounted in the calculations.
Please refer to EMSLearn for the dates when each online assessment is available.
Each AF quiz will be available for one week and will cover the content of a specific chapter . Students get one
attempt during the week for which they have one hour to complete the assessment.
Please note: B.Accounting students have an additional Linear Programming AF , which will form part of their
AFS2 mark. Note, only the lowest non-Linear Programming mark will be dropped in the calculations.

5.7 Pass requirements
AvalidFM ofat least 50is required to pass the module. Please take note in the calculation of final marks of
the requirement that thesum of weights needing to be at least one. This implies a penalty for missed main
assessments.
T o obtain avalid final markyou must:
•Complete at least one of A2S2 or A3
•Complete at least three main assessment opportunities
If you do not obtain avalidfinal mark, you will fail the module.
5.8 Access to Assessment 3 (A3)
Note that it is not necessary to submit medical certificates or any other documents for missed assessment
opportunities.
You have access to A3 if either of the following applies:
•If you completed all the main assessment opportunities other than A3 (A1S1, A2S1, A1S2, A2S2) but have
not obtained a FM of 50 or more, you have access to A3 in order to improve your FM to 50.
•You missed one or two main assessment opportunities (A1S1, A2S1, A1S2, A2S2) for whatever reason. In
this case, a FM of 50 or more can still be obtained if you make use of A3.
No further assessment opportunities will take place after A3.
If you passed the module after A2S2 and you have access to A3 (because you missed a main assessment)
you must inform the Registrar’s Office through the relevant procedure on SUNStudent of your intention to
use the A3 assessment opportunity. This notification must be submitted by no later than 48 hours after the
final marks after A2S2 have been released.
Take note:If you are a final-year student and make use of A3, graduation will only be possible in March/April
of the next academic year .
